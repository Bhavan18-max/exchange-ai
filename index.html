RBI Rules, KYC, AML & CFT

Key regulatory points for money changing in India, summarised for customers. Always confirm against the latest text on rbi.org.in.

Who may change money

  • Money changing is permitted only for Authorised Persons authorised by the RBI under Section 10 of FEMA, 1999.
  • No one may carry on or advertise money changing business without a valid RBI licence; violations attract penalties under FEMA.
  • Categories: Authorised Dealer Cat‑I (banks), AD Cat‑II, and Full Fledged Money Changers (FFMCs).

What an FFMC can do

  • Purchase foreign exchange from non‑residents visiting India and from residents.
  • Sell foreign exchange for private and business travel purposes only.
  • Franchisees, where permitted, are limited to restricted activities such as buying notes, coins and travellers’ cheques.

Master Direction – Money Changing Activities

  • RBI/FED/2015‑16/17 (FED Master Direction No. 3/2015‑16), regularly updated; consolidates instructions for FFMCs, non‑bank AD Cat‑II and franchisees.
  • Covers net owned fund, licensing, operational guidelines, reporting, audit and revocation.
  • Recent updates (2026) revised the FFMC framework and franchise arrangements — check the current text.

KYC

  • Non‑bank Authorised Persons must follow the RBI (NBFC – KYC) Directions, 2025 with effect from 28 Nov 2025; regulated entities follow their own KYC directions.
  • Customer identity is verified before a transaction is carried out, and records are maintained.
  • Documents needed depend on your residency, transaction type and purpose.

AML & CFT

  • Obligations flow from the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 and its rules.
  • Chapter VI of the Master Direction covers AML/KYC/CFT guidelines; franchisees must follow them too.
  • Suspicious transactions are monitored and reported as required.

Cash limit: ₹50,000

  • An Authorised Person may accept or pay cash only up to ₹50,000 per transaction against sale of foreign exchange for travel abroad.
  • If the amount is more than ₹50,000, payment must be made through a digital / banking channel: crossed cheque, banker's cheque, pay order, demand draft, debit/credit/prepaid card or electronic funds transfer.
  • Source: RBI A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 33 dated 24 Sep 2012, carried into the RBI Master Direction – Money Changing Activities. Confirm the current text on rbi.org.in.

Operational duties

  • Maintain Foreign Exchange (FLM) registers and file periodic returns to the RBI.
  • Sell‑to‑public condition: at least 75% of foreign currency purchased is to be sold to the public each quarter.
  • Concurrent audits, annual due diligence of franchisees and board‑level compliance oversight.
Customer tips: Deal only with an RBI‑authorised entity, ask for the licence number, insist on a printed receipt showing rate, charges and GST, and never hand over money to anyone offering rates that look too good to be true.
